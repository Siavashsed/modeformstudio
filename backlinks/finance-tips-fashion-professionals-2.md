## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How do fashion designers and models manage their finances when income is irregular and seasonal?

This is one of the biggest challenges in creative industries, and it's rarely discussed honestly. The core issue is that fashion income doesn't match regular living expenses—you might earn $20K in one month and nothing the next three months.

First, calculate your actual monthly burn rate. Most people overestimate how much they truly need monthly by including irregular expenses. Strip it back to essentials: rent, food, utilities, insurance, taxes. You'll usually find it's less than you thought.

Second, create a buffer account. When money comes in, the first step should be funding a 3-6 month emergency cushion that sits untouched. This transforms income volatility from terrifying to manageable. Once you have this buffer, seasonal income becomes a non-issue.

Third, set aside taxes immediately. This is non-negotiable as a freelancer or contract worker. A simple rule: if you're in a higher tax bracket, put 30-40% of each payment into a separate account before you spend anything else.

Fourth, establish baseline income sources if possible—retainers, teaching gigs, consulting work. Even small recurring revenue stabilizes everything else.

The professionals who thrive in fashion aren't necessarily earning more than their broke peers. They're just treating irregular income like a real business instead of hoping money will stretch.
