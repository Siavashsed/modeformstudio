## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How does the EU Textiles Strategy affect fashion designers and the fast fashion industry?

Short answer: it's the first time a major regulator has said openly that fast fashion's business model has to change, and designers are where much of that change will actually happen.

The European Commission published its Strategy for Sustainable and Circular Textiles on 30 March 2022. Its core idea is that by 2030 textiles sold in the EU should be durable, repairable and recyclable, made largely from recycled fibres, and produced with respect for social and environmental standards.

Here's what that means in practice for people who design clothes:

1. Durability becomes a design brief. Garments will be expected to last longer, so seam construction, fabric quality and fit matter more than speed to market.

2. Material choices get harder. Blends of several fibres are difficult to recycle, so designers will be pushed towards simpler, mono-material or easily separable constructions.

3. Digital product passports. Information about materials, origin and care is meant to travel with each product, which means designers and brands need to track it from the start.

4. Greenwashing gets riskier. Vague claims like "eco-friendly" will face tighter scrutiny, so any sustainability claim needs evidence behind it.

5. Unsold goods. Destroying unsold stock is targeted, which pushes brands to produce more carefully rather than overproduce and discard.

For fast fashion specifically, the model depends on very low prices, very high volume and short garment life. Each of the measures above raises the cost or lowers the speed of that model.

For models and stylists, fewer disposable collections generally means fewer high-volume shoots, with more emphasis on fewer, better-made pieces.
