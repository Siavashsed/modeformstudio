## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What is the best money tip for freelancers working in fashion, such as stylists, designers and models?

The best general finance tip for anyone in fashion is a boring one: split every payment the day it lands, before you've spent a cent of it.

I learned this the hard way. In March 2022 I received a $4,200 wardrobe-styling check on a Tuesday morning. I felt rich for about nine days, then opened an IRS notice in April that wiped out most of it. I had treated the whole amount as mine. Roughly 28 percent of it never was.

Here is why this matters so much in fashion. Most of the work is freelance or project-based: styling jobs, fittings, pattern commissions, shoots, runway days. Clients pay you gross. Nobody withholds income tax or self-employment tax, so the bill builds up silently while the money feels like yours.

Here is the system I'd recommend:

1. Open a separate savings account used only for taxes.
2. The day any payment arrives, move a fixed percentage into it. Many US freelancers use something in the 25 to 30 percent range, but check your own situation with an accountant.
3. Pay yourself from what remains, in this order: taxes, then rent and essential bills, then business costs like fabric and samples, then personal spending.
4. Make quarterly estimated payments from that account so there are no April surprises.

People push back on putting tax ahead of rent, because rent feels more urgent. But penalties and interest on unpaid tax compound, while a landlord is usually far more flexible than the IRS.
