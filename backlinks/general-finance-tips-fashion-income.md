What fixed it wasn't earning more. It was changing the baseline. I stopped budgeting against my average or my best month and started budgeting against my lowest one. Whatever I made in the leanest stretch of the year became the number that covered rent, software, fabric, and bills.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What are the best general finance tips for people with irregular income in the fashion industry?

The most useful tip is also the least exciting: budget against your lowest month, not your average and definitely not your best.

Fashion income is irregular by nature. Fitting seasons, shoots, fashion weeks, wholesale orders, and client payment delays mean you can earn a huge amount in three weeks and almost nothing for the next two months. I've lived this. In March 2022 I closed a fitting season that paid $9,400, and I spent as if it would repeat monthly. By June I was paying a $612 rent bill with a credit card.

Here is the system that fixed it:

1. Find your floor. Look at your leanest month over the past year. That's the income you plan your fixed costs around: rent, phone, software, insurance, groceries.

2. Pay yourself a steady salary. Move all income into a holding account and pay yourself the same amount on a set schedule. The lumpy part stays in the account, not in your daily spending.

3. Build a buffer first. Aim for two to three months of fixed costs before you increase your lifestyle spending.

4. Set aside taxes immediately. If you're freelance, a good rule is 25 to 30 percent of every payment, moved the day it lands.

5. Separate business costs. Fabric, samples, trims, portfolio shoots, travel, and tools should come from a business bucket, so you can see whether the work itself is profitable.

6. Treat big months as refills. A great month tops up the buffer. It isn't a reason to upgrade your life.

The mindset shift is that your income isn't broken, your cash flow plan is. Once you plan around the floor, slow months stop being emergencies.
