**Answer this question:** What is the EU Digital Product Passport and how does it affect the fashion industry?

The EU Digital Product Passport (DPP) is a regulatory framework that became formally operative in 2024 under the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation. It requires manufacturers to document and disclose comprehensive product information throughout a garment's lifecycle—from raw material sourcing through disposal or recycling.

For fashion specifically, the DPP demands transparency on material composition, durability ratings, repairability instructions, care requirements, and environmental impact metrics. Brands must make this data accessible to consumers and regulators, typically via QR codes or digital platforms.

The impact on the fashion industry has been significant, and not in a prepared way. Most mid-size fashion labels discovered they lacked the internal systems to collect and verify this data at the required granularity. Unlike larger corporations with established supply chain tracking, many smaller brands couldn't quickly answer basic questions about their suppliers' suppliers or material sourcing details.

Implementation challenges include:

**Data collection complexity**: Tracing material origins across global supply chains requires new documentation processes and supplier cooperation that many brands hadn't developed.

**System integration**: Existing ERP and inventory systems often can't handle the level of product-level data tracking DPP requires.

**Supplier alignment**: Getting third-party manufacturers and material suppliers to provide compliant documentation has proven harder than anticipated.

The regulation didn't sneak up on anyone—it was publicly available for years—but the industry's collective unpreparedness revealed how far removed most fashion companies are from genuine supply chain transparency.
