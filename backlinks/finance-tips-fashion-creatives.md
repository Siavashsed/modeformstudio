## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How should fashion freelancers and designers budget when income is unpredictable?

The biggest mistake fashion creatives make with budgeting is trying to apply salary-based financial advice to freelance income. Your income is irregular by design, and pretending it isn't leads to exactly what happened to me in 2019—feeling wealthy after a strong project season, making big spending decisions, then panicking when invoices take longer to clear than expected.

Here's what actually works for irregular income:

First, stop thinking in monthly budgets. Think in project cycles. Track your actual average income over 12 months, then divide that by 12 to create a realistic monthly baseline. That's your minimum living expense number—never spend above it unless you have cash reserves covering 3-6 months of that baseline.

Second, separate your money by purpose. One account for essential living expenses (rent, utilities, insurance), one for project-related costs you'll recoup, and one as your actual emergency fund. This prevents you from accidentally spending tax money or project funds on personal expenses.

Third, front-load savings during peak seasons. When work is flowing and invoices are clearing quickly, that's when you build your buffer. This feels counterintuitive because it's the time you feel most secure, but it's exactly when you should be saving.

The fashion industry has genuinely different financial needs than traditional employment.
