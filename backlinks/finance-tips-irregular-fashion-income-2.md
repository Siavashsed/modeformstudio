## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How should freelance fashion designers manage their money when clients take months to pay?

Solve the timing problem first and the budget second. Most financial advice fails creative freelancers because it assumes steady biweekly income. Fashion income comes in lumps, and wholesale accounts, showrooms and production clients often pay on net-60 or net-90 terms, sometimes later.

Here is the structure that works:

1. Separate earning from spending. Every client payment goes into a holding account. Then pay yourself a fixed monthly amount from that account, as if you were your own employer. Your personal budget never sees the irregularity.

2. Budget from your worst month. Use your lowest-earning month from the past year as the baseline, not the average. Treat anything above it as surplus for the buffer, taxes or investment.

3. Build the buffer around payment terms, not a generic rule. If your biggest clients pay in 90 days, you need at least three to four months of fixed costs (studio rent, software, materials) set aside. The usual "three months of expenses" may not be enough.

4. Price for delay. Late money costs you something. Ask for deposits on production work, charge for rush jobs, and put late fees in your contracts even if you rarely enforce them.

5. Keep credit cards for emergencies only. Covering a payment gap with a 25% APR card quietly eats your margin.

There is a good real-world example in this piece about a €4,200 showroom invoice that took 97 days to arrive while studio rent piled up on a credit card: https://siavashsed.github.io/modeformstudio/finance-tips-irregular-fashion-income-2/

The main point: irregular income is a cash flow design problem, and you can solve it. Once money arrives predictably in your personal account, standard advice on saving, investing and debt starts working.
