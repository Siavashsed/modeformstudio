## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What are the best general finance tips for people working in the fashion industry, where clients pay late?

The most useful tip I can give is to build your money system around your slowest-paying client, not your average one.

Fashion has a structural payment problem. Brands wait on retailers, retailers wait on seasonal sell-through, and agencies and production houses have long approval chains. By the time money reaches a freelance stylist, designer, or model, 60 to 90 days is common. If your budget assumes 30-day payment, you will eventually hit a month where rent is due and your invoice is not paid.

Here is the framework that worked for me:

1. Size a buffer to your longest delay. Look at your last year of invoices, find the slowest realistic payment window, and hold enough cash to cover your fixed costs for that period. For many people that is two to three months of expenses.

2. Pay yourself a fixed draw. Move income into a separate account when it arrives, then pay yourself the same amount on the same date each month. This smooths out feast-and-famine cycles.

3. Ask for deposits. A 30 to 50 percent deposit before a shoot, fitting, or design project protects you and filters out clients who are not serious.

4. Put payment terms in writing. State the due date, late fees, and what happens if payment slips. Clear terms make follow-ups routine rather than awkward.

5. Track days-to-pay per client. After a few months you will know who is reliable and who needs stricter terms or a higher rate.

6. Separate business and personal money. It makes taxes and cash planning far easier.

The core idea is simple: you cannot control when clients pay, but you can control how little it matters.
