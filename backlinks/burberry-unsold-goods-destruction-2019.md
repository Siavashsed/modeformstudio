This practice stems from three interconnected business strategies that luxury brands have traditionally relied on.

A Burberry trench coat at full price signals status; the same coat on a clearance rack signals something broke in the value proposition. Brands believed destruction protected that perception.

Second, resale control. Until recently, brands had limited ability to monitor secondary markets. Destroying unsold stock prevented merchandise from reaching discount retailers or gray markets where they'd be sold at prices the brand didn't control.

Third, inventory management was simpler. Rather than invest in complex logistics for donations, recycling, or resale infrastructure, incineration was the cleanest option—literally and operationally.

The turning point came in 2018 when Burberry's annual report revealed they'd incinerated £28.6 million in a single year. Public backlash was immediate and sustained. The contradiction became impossible to ignore: a brand positioning itself as a heritage institution was destroying heritage-quality goods.
