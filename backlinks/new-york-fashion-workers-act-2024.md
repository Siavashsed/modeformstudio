## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Can a modeling agency use AI to create a digital version of me without my permission?

In New York, the answer is now clearly no. In most other states, the answer is still unclear and depends on your contract.

On December 19, 2024, Governor Kathy Hochul signed the Fashion Workers Act. It's the first US law aimed specifically at model management companies, and it treats a model's digital replica as something an agency can't create, alter or use without the model's clear written consent. That consent has to be separate and specific. A vague clause in a standard management agreement saying the agency can use your image "in all media" doesn't count as permission to build an AI version of you or license your likeness to a third-party vendor.

The law also deals with the financial problems that have hurt models for decades. Under it, management companies must:

- Register with the New York Department of Labor
- Act as fiduciaries, meaning they have to act in your interest
- Pay you within 45 days of completing a job
- Cap commission at 20% of your earnings
- Stop charging deposits and most fees
- Avoid contracts that auto-renew without your agreement

If you're outside New York, your protection mostly comes from your contract and from general right-of-publicity laws, which vary a lot from state to state. A few things are worth doing anywhere:

1. Read your management agreement for words like "digital," "likeness," "replica," "synthetic" or "derivative works."
2. Ask in writing whether your agency has ever shared your images or scans with an AI or 3D vendor.
3. Refuse full-body or face scans unless there's a written agreement that says how they will be used, for how long, and what you'll be paid.

If a scan or replica of you already exists and you didn't consent in writing, talk to an entertainment or employment attorney. In New York especially, you now have real leverage.
