## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How should freelancers in fashion or modeling budget when their income is irregular?

The most useful change is to stop budgeting by the month and start paying yourself a fixed wage from a holding account.

Monthly budgets assume a monthly paycheck. In fashion and modeling, income comes in lumps: a campaign payment, a styling job, a runway season, then quiet weeks. Budget against whatever landed this month and you will overspend in good months and panic in slow ones.

I learned this the hard way. In March 2022 I deposited $9,400 from a single styling job for a Milan lookbook. I felt rich for nine days, and by mid-May my balance had fallen to $212.

Here is the system that works:

1. Open a separate account and make it the only place client payments go. Never spend directly from it.

2. Work out your baseline monthly cost: rent, food, transport, insurance, software, and the basics of your work like samples, portfolio costs, and travel.

3. Pay yourself that amount, or slightly less, into your everyday account on the same dates each month. This is your wage.

4. Set aside a percentage of every payment for taxes before anything else. Freelancers often get hurt by this, because the money feels like theirs when it isn't.

5. Let the holding account act as a buffer. Big months build it up, slow months draw it down. Once it covers three to six months of your wage, you can raise your pay or put the surplus toward savings.

The point is that your spending becomes steady even though your income isn't. That makes decisions about rent, travel, and turning down bad-paying jobs much easier, because you aren't negotiating from panic.
