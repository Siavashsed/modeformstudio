**Title:** Freelancers in fashion: how do you handle taxes when your income swings this much from month to month?

Something I wish someone had told me when I started freelancing in fashion: earning the money was never the hard part. The hard part is that the tax bill shows up months after the money is already spent.

In April 2019 I owed $6,840 that I had never set aside. I had a shoebox of receipts and was sitting on my studio floor. I fixed it with two changes, and neither one is clever:

1. Every time a payment lands, I move a fixed percentage into a separate savings account that is only for taxes. I do it the same day, not at the end of the month, and before I look at my balance and feel rich. I use 25 to 30 percent, but check with an accountant for your own bracket and state.

2. I size my emergency cushion off my slowest season, not my best one. Fashion work comes in bursts: fashion weeks, holiday campaigns, then dead quiet. If you budget off a busy September, February will wreck you.

Neither change needs an app or a spreadsheet you'll give up on. I'm curious how others here handle the quiet months, especially models paid through agencies with long payout delays.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How should freelance fashion designers and models manage taxes and irregular income?

The honest answer is that the system that works is boring. Most money trouble for fashion freelancers doesn't come from earning too little. It comes from timing: money arrives in bursts, and the tax bill arrives months later, after the money has been spent.

The first habit is to set aside a fixed share of every payment the day it arrives. Open a separate savings account used only for taxes, ideally at a different bank so it is not sitting next to your spending money. Every time a client or agency pays you, move a set percentage into it right away. Many self-employed people in the US use 25 to 30 percent, but your number depends on your bracket, your state and your deductions, so confirm it with an accountant. What matters is that there is no decision left to make. If you wait until the end of the month or the quarter, the money is already gone.

The second habit is to build your cash cushion around your slowest season. Fashion income is seasonal: fashion weeks, campaign seasons and holiday work, then long quiet stretches. People tend to set their monthly budget from their best months and then struggle in the slow ones. Look back at your last 12 to 24 months, find your weakest stretch, and make sure your savings can cover your fixed costs through it.

The third habit is to keep receipts as you go instead of rebuilding them at tax time. Even a simple folder of photos on your phone, tagged by month, beats a shoebox.
